# Quote collection: editorial candidates

Research date: 2026-11-20. Issue: [#9](https://github.com/mueller-k/stoic-block/issues/9).

## Status

The maintainer approved all eight scan-aligned excerpts below for the initial collection on 2026-11-22. On 2026-11-20 each passage was visually compared with a scan of a historical printed edition, with the discrepancies and limits recorded below. Long, Carter, and Gummere were checked in editions dated 1862, 1759, and 1917 respectively. Source links are for research, not runtime requests. No rights determination below is a claim of worldwide clearance.

The maintainer's editorial direction is eight excerpts, including at least one each from Marcus Aurelius, Epictetus and Seneca, without a fixed balance. Prefer readable, self-contained historical wording and a calm, constructive tone. Prefer complete consecutive sentences; permit a clearly marked internal omission only if it preserves meaning and materially improves readability. Settings should offer concise credits linked to complete bundled offline provenance.

## Approved excerpts

1. **Attention** — “Attend to the matter which is before thee, whether it is an opinion or an act or a word.” Marcus Aurelius, *Meditations* 8.22, translated by George Long. [Transcription and adjacent sections](https://classics.mit.edu/Antoninus/meditations.8.eight.html). A brief direction to attend to the immediate matter. Visually checked against Long’s 1862 edition, p. 129 (PDF page 211), where it is the first paragraph of section 22. The next paragraph is self-reproach for putting off becoming good; the excerpt excludes that sentence. This passage was the original development fixture.

2. **Purpose** — “Let no act be done without a purpose, nor otherwise than according to the perfect principles of art.” Marcus Aurelius, *Meditations* 4.2, translated by George Long. [Transcription and adjacent sections](https://classics.mit.edu/Antoninus/meditations.4.four.html). The phrase “perfect principles of art” may feel demanding or obscure; judge its tone in the blocked-visit setting. Visually checked as section 2 against Long’s 1862 edition, p. 40 (PDF page 122).

3. **Observation** — “Look within. Let neither the peculiar quality of anything nor its value escape thee.” Marcus Aurelius, *Meditations* 6.3, translated by George Long. [Transcription and adjacent sections](https://classics.mit.edu/Antoninus/meditations.6.six.html). A prompt to look carefully; its second sentence is somewhat abstract. Visually checked as section 3 against Long’s 1862 edition, p. 80 (PDF page 162).

4. **Acceptance** — “Require not Things to happen as you wish; but wish them to happen as they do happen; and you will go on well.” Epictetus, *Enchiridion* 8, translated by Elizabeth Carter. [Candidate transcription](https://livingstoicism.com/2023/05/12/the-enchiridion-or-manual-of-epictetus-by-elizabeth-carter-1758/). Consider whether this sounds too passive or dismissive of difficult circumstances when isolated. The 1759 scan has capital “Things” and semicolons; the candidate transcription had lowercase and commas. The wording above follows the scan (normalizing the long s and spacing).

5. **Small beginnings** — “Begin therefore from little Things.” Epictetus, *Enchiridion* 12, attributed to Elizabeth Carter in [this transcription](https://classics.mit.edu/Epictetus/epicench.html). In context, the advice concerns practicing equanimity over small losses, not a general prescription for productivity; assess whether the abbreviated sentence is misleading without that context. The 1759 scan capitalizes “Things”; the original candidate had lowercase. The wording above follows the scan (normalizing the long s and spacing).

6. **Today's work** — “Lay hold of to-day’s task, and you will not need to depend so much upon to-morrow’s.” Seneca, *Moral Letters to Lucilius* 1.2, translated by Richard M. Gummere. [Transcription and complete letter](https://en.wikisource.org/w/index.php?title=Moral_letters_to_Lucilius/Letter_1&oldid=14843822). Visually checked against Gummere’s 1917 edition, p. 3 (PDF page 32). The letter discusses time and mortality, including urgent language that is absent from this sentence. Consider whether the sentence feels supportive or pressuring in the product.

7. **Sustained attention** — “There is nothing so efficacious that it can be helpful while it is being shifted about.” Seneca, *Moral Letters to Lucilius* 2.3, translated by Richard M. Gummere. [Transcription and complete letter](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Moral_letters_to_Lucilius/Letter_2). Visually checked against Gummere’s 1917 edition, p. 7 (PDF page 36). The letter argues for reading deeply rather than moving constantly between authors; the sentence follows examples involving medicine and plants. “Efficacious” may be less approachable than the agreed readability standard.

8. **Shared learning** — “No good thing is pleasant to possess, without friends to share it.” Seneca, *Moral Letters to Lucilius* 6.4, translated by Richard M. Gummere. [Pinned transcription and complete letter](https://en.wikisource.org/w/index.php?title=Moral_letters_to_Lucilius/Letter_6&oldid=14843830). Seneca is discussing sharing knowledge and friendship; this offers a less solitary interpretation of growth. Visually checked, including the comma, against Gummere’s 1917 edition, p. 27 (PDF page 56).

The scan was used for source verification; the digitized Google file itself, its watermark, and its packaging are not proposed distribution assets.

For **each** Long and Carter excerpt, the reproduced translation is evidenced in a printed work published in 1862 or 1759 respectively. For **each** Gummere excerpt, the reproduced translation is evidenced in the printed 1917 edition, with both London and New York publishers identified on its title page. The US Copyright Office states in [Circular 22](https://www.copyright.gov/circs/circ22.pdf) that copyright in works published or copyrighted before 1931 has expired. Its [Circular 38B](https://www.copyright.gov/circs/circ38b.pdf) explains that restored foreign copyrights receive the remainder of the applicable US term; restoration does not create an indefinite term. These sources support a US public-domain rationale for the pre-1931 historical translations, not rights in any later additions. The scan comparison and source-specific dates are material to that rationale; ancient authorship or mere availability online would not suffice. No worldwide clearance is claimed.

No third-party transcription, ebook branding, license header, or scan images are required for the proposed manually collated historical text, so no such source-specific redistribution notice has been identified for those historical passages. This is conditional on bundling only those passages, with no later editorial additions; independently check the exact assets and notices in the eventual package. The project still requires full bundled provenance and attribution regardless of the copyright status.
